Enterprise Clinical Capability: Scaling Respiratory CME Across 800+ Hospital Staff

Client: King Fahad Medical City · Setting: Riyadh, Saudi Arabia · Governance: CH-DWC-STD-001 Compliant

01 · Clinical Challenge

Hospital audit data revealed that over 40% of patients discharged with persistent asthma returned to the emergency department within 90 days. Root-cause analysis indicated significant variation in how attending primary physicians and inpatient nurses instructed patients on inhaler technique and device maintenance. The hospital required a scalable, accredited training solution that would not compromise clinical shift coverage.

02 · Educational Approach

Credit Hours deployed the ProAir curriculum utilizing our C02 (Curriculum Design) and C05 (Digital LMS) capabilities. The program was designed in bite-sized, 15-minute asynchronous micro-modules that clinicians could access from their mobile phones between shifts, supported by interactive video simulations of patient-device counseling.

03 · Delivery & Accreditation

The program was accredited by the Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS) for 15 CME hours. Credit Hours handled registration, automated attendance tracking (enforcing the 90% completion threshold per regulatory guidelines), and direct batch synchronization with Mumaris Plus.

CH Perspective · Expert Review · 5 Min Read

“Why Generic E-Learning Fails Healthcare Professionals”

KA

Dr. Khalid Al-Mutairi, MD, FCCP

Consultant Pulmonologist & Medical Education Advisor, Riyadh

Verified Expert Disclosure: No commercial bias

Over the past decade, healthcare institutions across the Gulf have invested millions in digital learning platforms. Yet, clinical practice gaps persist. Why does high course completion fail to produce better bedside decisions?

The Flaw in Passive Information Delivery

Most LMS systems treat adult healthcare education as an exercise in information transfer. They convert 80-slide PowerPoint presentations into click-through digital modules. For a practicing physician or ICU nurse, this is immediately recognized as a compliance barrier rather than an educational asset.

Central Clinical Thesis

“Doctors do not learn from facts alone; they learn from navigating diagnostic uncertainty, comparing conflicting evidence, and observing the outcomes of clinical choices.”

What Truly Drives Engagement

Effective healthcare CME requires three essential elements:

  • Authentic clinical tension: Scenarios where the diagnosis is not obvious and requires differential reasoning.
  • Bite-sized micro-modules: Honoring clinician cognitive load by delivering 10–15 minute focused cases.
  • Frictionless accreditation: Immediate, reliable synchronization with licensing bodies like SCFHS (Mumaris Plus) and DHA.

E-LEARNING ATTENDANCE

E-Learning Attendance Policy

Attendance, completion and virtual-learning capacity requirements for CREDIT HOURS activities.

On this page
Validity of AttendanceAttendance CalculationVirtual Learning CapacityCompliance and Monitoring
01

Validity of Attendance

Learners must attend during the accreditation period. Registration, opening and closing activities, guidance, discussions, Q&A and communication activities are excluded from the educational attendance-hour calculation.

02

Attendance Calculation

  • Asynchronous, regular, concurrent virtual and blended activities require at least 90% of the full educational hours.
  • Blended activities additionally require at least 25% of the full e-learning hours.
  • Virtual attendance is calculated as regular attendance for this purpose.
03

Virtual Learning Capacity

The standard capacity is 25 trainees per trainer. A 25% increase may be permitted depending on course type, activities and interaction, and trainer experience. For 26–50 trainees, an assistant trainer is added; above 50, an assistant trainer is provided per 50 trainees. There is no upper limit for asynchronous programs lasting less than one month.

04

Compliance and Monitoring

Activities should not exceed the permitted trainee capacity. LMS management monitors entry and electronic attendance and reports periodically to trainers. The center informs trainees of their attendance rates.
